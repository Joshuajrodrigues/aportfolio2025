# Why design a magazine?
Magazines have always been an integral part of my life—and of so many kids and adults like me. I have fond memories of my mother and me reading together every evening. She would read her magazines, and I’d read my children’s magazines. There was a certain welcoming quality to holding a magazine that catered to your interests, even as a child.

Not to mention, physical media is fast disappearing—but that has never discounted the reasons why it deserves to be a part of every human’s life. Regardless of trends and loss of interest, physical magazines—and even their limitations, like missing out on quick trends due to the time it takes to produce them—are exactly why we need them now more than ever.

Even with ten thousand recommendations at our fingertips every second of every day, we are never any closer to discovering artists who are interesting, wild, free, and making amazing music beneath the ginormous pressure and noise of trends. Those are the voices that encouraged me to work on a magazine for artists I believe in.

(Years after I made this magazine, it brings me great joy to see that the artists I featured here are still thriving and reaching greater heights.)

# Straying away from the mainstream, with design

For the design of the magazine, I wanted to let the spirit of straying from the mainstream take the wheel. When it comes to the colors and the layouts, I wanted to follow the philosophy that less is more—while also allowing the concept of rest to enter the space. Every page being covered in text would overwhelm even the calmest of music enthusiasts.

Using green is an attempt to grab the attention of readers passing by a stall and help them associate it easily with certain streaming giants that have already solidified their identities through branding. The hope is that many people can comfortably associate music with a similar color profile they already interact with every day. But within the magazine—through its text layout, the use of artist images, and imagery that highlights their albums—ActOut stands on its own two feet.

Understanding the artists through their music and the messages they want to convey is the magazine’s strength, and that is, in fact, the reason why our recommendations aren’t made lightly. The magazine believes in the artists and brings them to you every month. Every design decision, quote, and image used is brought onto the page with the artist’s intention in mind, as well as the magazine’s purpose.
