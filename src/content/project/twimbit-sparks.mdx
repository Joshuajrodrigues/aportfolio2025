# Overview

Twimbit wanted custom merchandise for an upcoming company-wide team offsite.

Rather than treating the request as routine corporate swag, I pitched and led an initiative to build a culture-focused brand asset called Sparks.

# The Hiccup

Standard corporate merchandise often ends up disposable, lacking narrative connection to the team or long-term brand equity.

The initial concept was abstract, requiring buy-in from all co-founders before physical production and vendor procurement could begin.

# The Idea

Sparks frames the employee growth journey through a four-stage visual narrative, utilizing hibiscus and spark motifs across wearable apparel.

The lineup encompassed graphic tees, hoodies, sweatpants, tote bags, and an accompanying six-page editorial zine that articulated the story behind the collection.

# The Process

I built and pitched the concept deck directly to the executive leadership team to secure budget and scope approval.

I then directed two designers through iterative apparel illustration, managed vendor sourcing across India and China for garment sampling, and prepared production-ready asset packages.

# The Impact

The project delivered a cohesive merchandise ecosystem with completed physical prototypes and print-ready packaging assets.

The collection established an internal design precedent for using physical goods to reinforce company culture and milestone recognition.
