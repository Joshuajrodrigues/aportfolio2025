# Merchandise That Unites and Inspires
The Sparks project began as a simple merchandise design request for an upcoming internal team offsite. But I saw an opportunity to turn it into something more meaningful—an experience that could spark dialogue around growth, identity, and inspiration across the company.

I pitched the idea of transforming the merchandise into a long-term, memorable brand asset—one that, if successful internally, could also evolve into a way to engage with clients. At the verbal discussion stage, the concept felt abstract and difficult to grasp, but I was passionate about its potential. I asked the stakeholders for the chance to present a full pitch. Though nerve-wracking—especially since the audience included all of the co-founders—I was excited to put in the extra effort to translate my vision into a clear, actionable pitch deck for the team.

Sparks was conceived as a visual representation of the growth and innovative spirit of Twimbit’s employees. It was structured into four stages, each symbolizing a different phase in an employee’s journey—from new joiners to seasoned team members whose ideas and influence ripple outward through the company and beyond. This evolution was represented through illustrated elements of a hibiscus flower, a spark, and other design motifs. The aesthetic leaned toward a bold, youthful energy, and the designs were applied to items like graphic t-shirts, hoodies, sweatpants, and more—balancing symbolism with wearability and appeal.

# Covering Ground and Bringing the Idea Together

I was joined by a talented designer who had joined us recently and would take over the illustrations and together we came up with iterations upon iterations till we knew we had something special. With input at every stage from the stakeholders we knew it was headed in a direction that was faithful to the original idea in my head but also to the business ambitions and limitations. We finally put together a version we were happy with and wanted it to go into the sampling process. I even had the chance to do some of the calling out to vendors in India and China myself. And at the end we had some options locked and loaded for t-shirts and hoodies to go along with a tote bag. The experience of the packaging would also be added to by a small 6 page zine that would encapsulate the entire Sparks story into it and attach the story to the employees time at Twimbit.


# In Conclusion

The journey of Sparks was truly a great experience as a designer. From getting the opportunity to pitch the concept to working on the deliverables, every step of the way was exciting and built my knowledge base. Merchandising design is always an exciting prospect but to get a chance to truly build an experience with it was even more thrilling.
