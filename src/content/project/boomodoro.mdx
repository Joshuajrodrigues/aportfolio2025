# A timer that can focus on rest

The modern workday has often rewarded people’s ability to stay focused and enduring physical or mental discomfort to achieve just one more task. Desk-work is rarely predictable and often works through waves and rhythms. These changes are rarely accounted for in time-management timers that are often relied upon by people wanting more structure. The existing timers focus on fixed work cycles, streaks, gamified scores and reminders as ways to improve the work experience. People now, more than ever want to focus on their rest and restorative activities as much as they do on their work and chores. Fatigue, posture issues, lack of focus, pain and minimal attention to hydration and food has affected people more than ever. Extended period of sitting at a desk, engrossed in a task can feel necessary when the task list grows, but rest and rhythm are what truly helps in the sustainability of a persons work. The gap between existing timers and a need for prioritizing rest led to Boomodoro.

# Why this project ?

Time-management and productivity tools are widely used but most are geared toward a certain style of work that has increasingly disenchanted me and many of my fellow desk-workers. Through that personal note, I became interested in this topic and found that there was a larger opportunity to support those wanting to prioritize rest over endurance, rhythm over intensity.
</SideImage>

# The Hiccup

How can we help people create a more sustainable work-rest transition without turning rest into another productivity task?

# The Research

Through Diary Studies and Interviews, the following insights emerged.

<Insights>
  <Insight icon="/icons/completion.svg">Work completion appears to act as a psychological boundary for breaks</Insight>
  <Insight icon="/icons/discomfort.svg">Physical discomfort can become the mechanism that prompts movement</Insight>
  <Insight icon="/icons/workflow.svg">Break opportunities are sometimes fit within the workflow</Insight>
  <Insight icon="/icons/guilt.svg">Guilt about not meeting rigid restorative goals can add to work overwhelm as well</Insight>
</Insights>

</Band>

<Band bg="green" icon="/icons/idea.svg">
# The Idea

People should be able to actively include rest into their workday without making it just another task to keep track of. Introducing rhythm, flexibility and taking away the guilt in taking a break (or not taking it the ‘right way’) can help desk-workers work sustainably.
</Band>
