# My Role & Responsibilities

**Worldbuilding & Narrative Design** : Co-created the story for the game to develop the core idea into a comprehensive narrative

**Visual Direction** : Defined the visual theme and tone for the world and characters through moodboarding, inspiration sourcing, and thematic exploration to establish a consistent aesthetic

**Game Flow and UX Integration** : Applied UX principles to build intuitive game flows and player interactions 

**Game Design Research** : Conducted research into platformer games and utilized industry examples to inform the design process

**Character & Environment Concept Design** : Created and iterated over character and environment concept design, background images and aesthetic to suit the games narrative


# The Story

CrashingHome is about the journey of finding your way back home. An alien is stuck in a pod in space which unfortunately runs out of fuel and crashes onto a planet. The alien and our main character, Lumen, finds that his memory is lost and everything in front of him is unfamiliar. The spirit of Lumen is bright and his curiosity and openness helps him to engage with this unfamiliar world and maybe even find his home.

There were plenty of late-nights spent in brainstorming for this story that slowly developed from our conversations. The theme of home and what it means to have to lost some sense of it began to emerge just out of the writing process and we were able to add the layers required to make this into a game.

# Game Design though UX Thinking
Though we were unfamiliar with game design as a whole, we wanted to approach it with the skills we have and figure out the rest of the way through. I began the process by studying the greats and relying on an amazing platform game called Hollow Knight. The game served as a basis of study and helped me to map out the screens we’d need to build our game. This would serve as a breakthrough for me as I started to connect the dots of how UX can be applied to game making. 

The rough draft of the flow of screens is shown here. This draft was a result of applying UX processes such as conducting research and visualizing the user flow. There was also a lot of brainstorming involved to ideate a solution that would assign the right screens for the right action points in the actual narrative of the game’s story. There was a certain amount of story boarding involved as well to help us set off in the right direction for the next steps which were visual direction and theme exploration.
</p>

# Visual Direction and Inspiration

To narrow down the visual language we wanted, I began by creating moodboards. To convey the visual direction and tone of the story, I assembled examples of game art, menu screens, and interface references that aligned with our scope and could help us visualize the game flow. The theme we arrived at was moody and surreal, set in a 2D world with primarily hand-drawn game art. The environments would combine atmospheric and vibrant elements, supporting a more reflective narrative that allows the player to feel hopeful for the main character as the story unfolds.

# In Conclusion
Working on a game is a uniquely immersive experience. It demands compelling storytelling and captivating worldbuilding while also requiring strong UX design and development processes to bring it all together. Designing a game that was truly made from scratch with no prior experience meant that every step was a learning opportunity. While it was challenging, it has also been tremendously rewarding. Though we have a ways to go to make this game a playable experience, we are extremely hopeful that this game has the potential to be someones favourite game.
