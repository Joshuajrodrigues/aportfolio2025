# The zine design process
Since this was more of a personal project, I wanted it to be the most fun I could have while designing. I made sure to listen closely to the song and watch the music video. Given that the Korean music industry includes such talented visual artists who dedicate immense thought to the visuals behind these songs, I decided to take their lead. The music video and the behind-the-scenes footage posted by the artist herself were very helpful in narrowing down the aesthetic.

The music video is composed of sweeping white, snowy landscapes and the artist dressed in a white gown. There are pastel color themes in the abandoned swimming pool and house sets, which enhance the feeling of a time we’re nostalgic for. Since the presence of snow was significant—as well as the themes of isolation and heartbreak—I decided to keep the zine in black and white.

Korean is also a language I’m deeply fascinated by and have recently begun learning, which added to my resolve to feature many of the original Korean lyrics, along with short written interpretations I wanted to express. The combination of text and the original language lyrics creates a nostalgic feel. The choice of paper also plays a huge role in this—a glossy, shiny paper wouldn’t suit the tone. A more regular, grainy paper would be the ideal choice. And with all these componants in mind, this zine became a beautiful reminder to me of a song I enjoyed so much.
