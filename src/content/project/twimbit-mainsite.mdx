# A Truly Evolutionary Journey
This task demanded not only the skills of an in-house designer, but also the mindset of a strategic thinker. In essence, the team needed someone who could navigate the fast-paced nature of a growing startup while understanding the unique purpose of the brand. As the sole UI/UX designer, I took ownership of the end-to-end design process, adapting to a wide range of responsibilities to ensure progress and delivery.

Despite evolving requirements, tight deadlines, and the first few launches taking place within an inherited design system, we saw immediate improvements. After two major design evolutions, **event occurrences across the website increased by over 200%**. These changes significantly improved user engagement and helped position Twimbit’s use case more clearly to clients—making the platform easier to navigate and the messaging far more effective.

# From Systems to Screens

Ultimately, we made the decision to revamp the entire design system and branding of the company. I was actively involved in this process, collaborating with a Malaysia-based design firm to help align our new design philosophy with stakeholder input.

Using this refined philosophy and updated design system, I went on to build dozens of pages and microsites, led the internal rollout of the rebrand, and even mentored new design recruits to help them get up to speed with Twimbit’s design culture. As the business needs and vision evolved with time, I also proposed and implemented structural improvements across the website to better support those shifts

# In Conclusion

In the end, the website came to closely reflect the stakeholders’ vision and messaging. Our iterative approach—taking time to understand our true position in the industry and acting strategically—was what ultimately helped us achieve that alignment.

By the end of my time at Twimbit, the main website was running on autopilot, with several microsites either launched or ready to go live. This collection of websites became a deeply formative experience for me. Managing the messaging, tone, and vision across multiple platforms—balancing the collective identity with the individual arc of each story—taught me how to lead with clarity and perspective. It helped me step into the role of someone who could hold the bird’s-eye view of a complex, multi-page system.
